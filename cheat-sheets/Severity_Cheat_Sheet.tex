{\huge{\textbf{\texttt{Severity} Class Cheat Sheet}}}

\raggedright
\texttt{\m Severity(sev\_name, exp\_attachment=None, exp\_limit=inf, sev\_mean=0, sev\_cv=0, sev\_a=nan, sev\_b=0, sev\_loc=0,} \\
\texttt{\phantom{\m Severity(}sev\_scale=0, sev\_xs=None, sev\_ps=None, sev\_wt=1, sev\_lb=0, sev\_ub=inf, sev\_conditional=True, name='')} \\

A \texttt{Severity} wraps a frozen \texttt{scipy.stats} rv (in \texttt{fz}) for a single ground-up loss, optionally cut to a layer by the attachment / limit clause. Mixtures and the \texttt{wt} weight are handled by \texttt{Aggregate}.
The following tables show all \texttt{\m methods}, and fields or properties (used interchangeably). Comments elucidate the meaning of more obscure entries.

\bigskip \raggedright
{\bf Notes:}

A \texttt{Severity} models one ground-up severity curve; frequency, mixing, and reinsurance live on \texttt{Aggregate}. \texttt{sev\_mean}+\texttt{sev\_cv} or \texttt{sev\_a}(+\texttt{sev\_b}) parameterise the shape; \texttt{sev\_loc}/\texttt{sev\_scale} then shift/scale.

Abbreviations: m=mean, cv=coefficient of variation, sd=standard deviation, var=variance, skew(ness).

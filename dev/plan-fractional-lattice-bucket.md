# Plan: an exact bucket size for discrete severities on a fractional lattice

`[Fractional-Lattice-Bucket]`. Drafted 2026-10-28 against `dev` at 1.19.0 (`8f75495`), from the
PMIR project (`D:\Projects\PMIR`), for the aggregate session. **Status: draft. The design questions
below are open and are settled with Steve before anything is executed.**

## Goal

When every severity atom is a whole multiple of a common step, choose `bs` so that each atom lands
exactly on a grid point and the grid is sized to the support, for fractional and signed atoms as
already happens for integer atoms. One lattice-step routine serves the univariate sizer, the
Portfolio, the bivariate severity and the renewal grid.

## Why

A discrete severity on a lattice has an aggregate on the same lattice, whatever the frequency, so
an exact and small grid is available. Today aggregate finds it only when the atoms are integers.
The first example below, three atoms at quarter points, gets a grid about 2,000 times larger than
it needs.

The immediate user is PMIR, the book *Pricing Multi-period Insurance Risk* and its package `pmir`
(`V:\dev\PMIR`). Its DMC pricing method sums the mean-zero increments of a Doob martingale, which
are signed fractions, as a Portfolio of signed `dsev` units. The legacy PMIR package ran its own
FFT convolution partly to get an exact grid for these sums; the rule for `pmir` is that grids are
aggregate's job.

## Current behavior

Measured on `dev` at 1.19.0 (and identically on the 1.11.0 release) with
`build('agg T 3 claims dsev [atoms] [.25 .5 .25] fixed')`:

| atoms | `_severity_lattice()` | `bs` | `log2` |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 0.25, 0.5, 1.75 | `None` | $2^{-13}$ | 16 |
| -1.75, 0.25, 0.5 | `None` | $2^{-13}$ | 16 |
| 3, 6, 9 | 3.0 | 1 | 5 |
| 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 | `None` | $2^{-16}$ | 16 |

- `Aggregate._severity_lattice` (`_aggregate.py`, line 6975) returns the gcd of the atoms when all
  are integers (to `atol=1e-9`) and `None` otherwise. `_bucket_window._sharpen_lattice` and
  `_bucket_is_exact` (lines 2057 and 2073) delegate to it, taking an integer gcd across units for a
  Portfolio, and the sizer `_size` (line 962) uses the result as `lattice_bs`.
- Quarter-point atoms therefore fall to the generic estimator: $2^{-13}$ divides 0.25, so the grid
  is exact by accident, but 65,536 buckets cover a support that needs about 22 at `bs = 0.25`.
- Decimal atoms are not multiples of $2^{-16}$, so each is spread over two buckets.
- For 3, 6, 9 the lattice is found but `bs = 1` is chosen; the grid is exact, three times finer
  than the lattice.
- Two more lattice routines exist: `bivariate._lattice_bs` (line 1169), a floating-point gcd for
  `dbvsev` axes, and `_renewal._lattice_step` (line 287), a gcd of
  `Fraction.limit_denominator(10**9)` approximations verified to `1e-9` relative.
- Policy that constrains any change: `utilities.round_bucket` (line 236) keeps a sub-unit `bs` a
  power of two, deliberately binary exact ("0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 are not"), and `snap_bs_to_reference`
  (`_bucket_window.py`, line 712) keeps the grid commensurable with a referenced object's `d` by
  snapping to $d \cdot 2^m$.

## The legacy algorithm

`D:\Projects\PMIR\Python\pmir_package\src\pmir_legacy\working.py`: `dyadic_step_exact` and
`dyadic_step_tol`, with the helpers `_gcd_many` and `_ctz`. Read-only reference; the code is marked
there as slated to move here.

- **Exact.** Every float is exactly a dyadic rational $p_i / 2^{k_i}$ (`float.as_integer_ratio`).
  Put all of them over $2^K$, $K = \max k_i$, take the gcd $g$ of the integer numerators and cancel
  its factors of two. The step $b = m / 2^h$, $m$ odd, generates the additive group generated by the
  atoms, so every $x_i / b$ is an integer in exact binary arithmetic.
- **Tolerant.** The smallest $h$ such that every $x_i$ lies within `tol` of $2^{-h}\mathbb Z$
  (optionally all but a fraction `robust_frac`), followed by the same gcd reduction.

On the examples above, and on $1/3, 2/3$:

| atoms | exact | tolerant, `tol=1e-6` |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 0.25, 0.5, 1.75 | 0.25 | 0.25 |
| -1.75, 0.25, 0.5 | 0.25 | 0.25 |
| 3, 6, 9 | 3 | 3 |
| 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 | $2^{-55}$ | $2^{-19}$ |
| 1/3, 2/3 | 1/3 (as a float, height 54) | $2^{-19}$ |

Both find only dyadic lattices. Decimal atoms defeat them; the rational step of
`_renewal._lattice_step` recovers $1/10$ and $1/3$.

## Proposed change

Subject to the design questions; each step a version bump.

1. **[Lattice-Step-Routine]** One routine returning the step, the method that found it and the
   largest residual, or `None` when there is no useful lattice. It tries, in order, the exact
   binary step, the rational step, and the tolerant binary step.
2. **[Severity-Lattice-Fractional]** `_severity_lattice` uses it, and `_sharpen_lattice` takes the
   gcd across Portfolio units as a rational gcd of steps rather than an integer gcd. The bucket
   table's `exact_discrete` row ("the exact discrete (integer-lattice) support") names the lattice
   and the method.
3. **[Lattice-Routines-Consolidated]** `bivariate._lattice_bs` and `_renewal._lattice_step` call
   the routine, with their results unchanged.

**Acceptance.**

- The first two examples get `bs = 0.25` and `log2` of at most 6, every atom on a grid point.
- A signed Portfolio of three units with dyadic `dsev` atoms gets the gcd of their steps.
- The integer examples are unchanged.
- The suite passes. Over `src/aggregate/agg/reproductions.agg` and `recitations.agg`, only programs
  with fractional `dsev` atoms change, and each change is listed and checked.

## Design questions

**[Decimal-Lattices]** Whether to allow a bucket size that is not binary exact. This is the central
question. Exact atoms need `bs` to divide 0.1, and no binary fraction does, so for decimal atoms
exact atoms and exact grid arithmetic conflict: `3 * 0.1` is `0.30000000000000004`, which matters
wherever a grid point is looked up by value. Options:

- (a) dyadic lattices only, with decimal atoms as today;
- (b) `bs` the rational step as a float, with grid labels rounded to the lattice's precision;
- \(c) an integer grid in units of the step, with a scale applied to labels.

Recommendation: (a) now, since it fixes the large miss within the current policy. (b) or \(c)
follows as a separate plan, after an audit of where aggregate relies on a binary-exact `bs`.

**[Lattice-As-Bucket]** Whether `bs` is the lattice step itself or a divisor of it.

- A step of 0.75 is binary exact but not a power of two: `bs = 0.75` honors the lattice, while
  `bs = 0.25` honors `round_bucket`'s power-of-two rule at three times the grid.
- Settle the integer case at the same time: why 3, 6, 9 gets `bs = 1` rather than 3, and whether
  the lattice is meant as the bucket or only as a constraint on it.

**[Lattice-Fits-Grid]** When a lattice is too fine to use. An exact binary step on computed data
can have height near 55. Decide the rule, presumably that span / step must fit the `log2` cap, and
how it relates to the existing `grow_cap` logic in `_size`.

**[Lattice-Tolerance]** How tolerant to be by default.

- Atoms computed by arithmetic, such as conditional means, carry rounding noise, so the exact step
  is useless for them, and the tolerant step moves each atom by up to `tol`.
- Decide between an absolute and a relative tolerance, the default (exact only, or a tiny relative
  value such as $10^{-12}$), and how the residual is reported.
- `robust_frac`, which lets some atoms miss the lattice, defeats exactness; the recommendation is to
  drop it.

**[Signed-Lattice-Origin]** On a signed or windowed grid, the origin must itself be a multiple of
`bs` for atoms to land on grid points. Confirm that the window origin snaps to the lattice.

Atoms such as 0.1, 1.1, 2.1 lie on a shifted lattice, $0.1 + \mathbb Z$. Only a fixed claim count
benefits from that, since a random count mixes the shifts; the recommendation is the linear lattice
(the gcd with 0 included).

**[Lattice-Portfolio]** A Portfolio takes the rational gcd of its units' steps, and any continuous
unit means no lattice, as now.

**[Lattice-Numbers-Move]** `bs` changes for every program with fractional `dsev` atoms, so its
numbers move (to the exact answer). This needs a minor bump, a "numbers move" sentence in the
CHANGELOG, and the corpus check above. Decide whether any opt-out setting is wanted; the
recommendation is none.

**[Lattice-Pinned-Bucket]** A `bs` the user pins is left alone, as `snap_bs_to_reference` does.
Decide whether a pinned `bs` that misses the lattice warns.

**[Lattice-Routine-Name]** Public or private, and its name, vetted against the existing surface as
this repository's `CLAUDE.md` requires (`_renewal._lattice_step` exists and is replaced by it).
